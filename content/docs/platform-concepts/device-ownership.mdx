---
title: "Device ownership and transfer"
description: "What owns a device, what travels with it when it moves, what is deliberately detached, and why a transfer never touches the device itself."
---

A device belongs to exactly one **workspace**. Everything else about its tenancy
is derived from that one column, which is why moving a device is a single
operation rather than a migration.

## What owns a device

There is no `organizationId` on a device. A device points at a workspace, a
workspace points at an organization, and every authorization decision walks that
chain:

```
organization ──> workspace ──> device
```

The consequence is worth stating plainly, because the API's shape depends on it:

<Callout title="Moving between workspaces and moving between organizations are the SAME operation">
  Both are one write to the device's `workspaceId`. What differs is the blast
  radius, not the mechanism. A transfer that happens to land in a workspace of
  another organization is flagged `crossOrg: true` and runs an extra cleanup
  pass; nothing else about it is special.
</Callout>

That is also why the destination is always named as a **workspace**.
[`POST /devices/{id}/transfer`](/api-reference/devices/transfer-device) takes a
`workspaceId`, and an `organizationId` only to prove you meant to cross an
organization boundary.

## What moves with the device

Everything the platform stores about the device, because it is all keyed by
device id rather than by tenant:

|                           |                                                                        |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **All telemetry history** | Every reading ever ingested, and the latest-value view.                |
| **All recorded footage**  | Every media session, every segment, and the objects behind them.       |
| **The device's identity** | Its AWS IoT thing, its X.509 certificate, its role alias.              |
| **Its video channels**    | The KVS signalling channels, re-tagged to the new tenant.              |
| **Its configuration**     | The config document and its revision.                                  |
| **Its device model**      | The hardware catalog is platform-wide, so the model survives any move. |

<Callout type="warn" title="For a cross-organization transfer this is a data-disclosure event">
  History is **never purged** on transfer, and a transfer has no option to purge
  it. The new owner can read every reading the device ever published and play
  back every second it ever recorded, including everything collected while you
  owned it.

This is deliberate: a device's history is the device's, and silently dropping
it would make a handover lossy in a way nobody could audit. If history must
not travel, [purge it](#purging-device-data) before you transfer: a separate,
destructive operation you perform knowingly.

[`GET /devices/{id}/transfer/preview`](/api-reference/devices/preview-device-transfer)
returns `carriesHistory: true` so a confirmation dialog can say this out loud
before anyone agrees to it. It is always `true`; render it, do not branch on
it.

</Callout>

## What stays behind

Three things remain with the source organization, because they are records of
the device _under its former owner_ rather than properties of the device:

- **The registration record.** How this device came to exist, and in whose
  workspace. Its `deviceId` is retained, but the record does not move.
- **Completed workflow runs.** What the device did while the old owner owned it,
  including the run history and its events.
- **Dataset items** built from its footage. Datasets are organization-scoped;
  the frames were collected and annotated under the old organization, and the
  dataset stays there.

## Purging device data

A transfer moves history and never removes it. The companion action is a
**purge**:
[`POST /devices/{id}/purges`](/api-reference/devices/create-device-purge) wipes
what a device has accumulated while keeping the device itself enrolled. Use it
to clean a bench device before it goes to a customer, to drop what a device
collected before a cross-organization handover, or to get rid of junk without
re-provisioning. Deleting the device is the other option, and it destroys the
identity too.

What goes is chosen per `scope`, and the three combine in one request:

|             | Removed                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Kept                                                             |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `workflows` | Every attached workflow is detached; every production run of this device, including runs of workflows detached earlier, goes with its steps, events and S3 artifacts. Runs still executing are stopped. | The organization's workflow templates and their simulation runs. |
| `telemetry` | Recorded telemetry sessions, their segments and overview artifacts, the ingest log, and every stored reading.                                                                                           | The latest-value view the fleet roster reads.                    |
| `media`     | Recorded video sessions, their segments and thumbnails.                                                                                                                                                 | The live video channels.                                         |

Whatever the scopes, the device row, its IoT identity and certificate, its
configuration, its webhook routes and its latest-value cache stay, and the
agent keeps running. Dataset items built from its footage are copies under the
dataset and stay too. Everything older than the moment of the request (the
purge's `cutoff`) is removed and what the device uploads afterwards is kept, so
a recording in progress is cut at that point: stop recording first. There is no
undo.

**Owner or admin**, and organization-wide like a transfer: a workspace-scoped
session token is refused with `403 WORKSPACE_SCOPED` whatever its role. A
device outside the caller's reach is a `404`, never a `403`.

**It is asynchronous.** The request answers `202` with a purge row in
`status: "queued"`, and a worker deletes in resumable chunks. Poll
[`GET /devices/{id}/purges/{purgeId}`](/api-reference/devices/get-device-purge)
every few seconds until `status` is `succeeded` or `failed`; `counts` says what
has gone so far and `cursor.step` where the job is. One purge per device at a
time (`409 PURGE_IN_PROGRESS`), and none while a transfer offer for the device
is pending (`409 TRANSFER_PENDING`), because the handover detaches attachments
itself.
[`GET /devices/{id}/purges/preview`](/api-reference/devices/preview-device-purge)
returns the counts a confirmation dialog should show, and
[`GET /devices/{id}/purges`](/api-reference/devices/list-device-purges) is the
audit trail: who purged what, and when.

From `@xorgate/sdk` 0.10.0 the same routes are
[`xg.devices.purges`](/backend-sdk/resources/devices#purge-data).

## What is broken on purpose

### Workflow attachments to the old organization's templates

Workflow templates are organization-scoped; an attachment joining this device to
one is not. Left alone, a transferred device would keep executing a template its
new owner cannot see and its old owner no longer controls.

So on a cross-organization transfer, **attachments are detached**, and the
response names them:

```json
{
  "transfer": {
    "crossOrg": true,
    "detachedWorkflowAttachments": ["Perimeter intrusion", "Idle engine alert"]
  }
}
```

They are named rather than counted so a confirmation can list exactly what will
stop running. Templates are never copied into the destination — that would
duplicate another organization's work without being asked.

A same-org transfer detaches nothing: the templates are still visible from the
new workspace, so `detachedWorkflowAttachments` is `[]`.

### Credentials scoped to the old workspace

An API key or session token scoped to the source workspace stops resolving the
device the moment it moves. Nothing is revoked — the credential is still valid
for everything else in its workspace — but this device is no longer in it.

For a REST caller that shows up as a `404`, not a `403`. Confirming that another
tenant's resource exists would itself be a leak, so "you cannot see this" and
"this does not exist" are deliberately indistinguishable.

For a **live** consumer it is worse, because nothing fails at all: the telemetry
socket stays connected and simply goes quiet. If you build on the live plane,
read
[Scope and transfers](/frontend-sdk/device-scope) before you ship — that silence
is the single worst failure mode this feature has.

### In-flight work is left alone

Running workflow runs are counted in the preview and left to finish. Open media
sessions keep uploading, because segments are keyed by device and land under the
new owner. Neither blocks a transfer.

## A transfer does not touch the device

**Nothing on the device changes, so a device does not have to be online to be
transferred.**

- Its certificate and identity are untouched. The thing name, certificate and
  role alias all derive from the device id, and the IoT policy carries no
  literal tenancy at all — it interpolates the thing's attributes at connect
  time. A transfer re-tenants a device by editing the **cloud registry**, not
  the box.
- Its tenancy is never written to disk. The device learns which telemetry topic
  to publish on from a retained MQTT message, and holds it in memory only.
- The retained message waits. The broker replays it on the device's next
  connect, so a device that was offline through a transfer converges the moment
  it comes back.

<Callout title="Offline is a normal success, not an error">
  Transferring an offline device returns `200` with a warning in
  `transfer.warnings`, never a `4xx`. No reboot, no site visit, no
  re-provisioning, and nothing to retry.

If anything, transferring while offline is the _safer_ case: there is no
running agent holding a stale topic, so the device boots, reads the retained
config and starts on the correct topic first time.

</Callout>

## There is no delivery receipt

Ownership changes immediately. Whether the **device** has noticed is a separate
question, and the API keeps them separate rather than pretending otherwise.

The moment `POST /devices/{id}/transfer` returns `200`, the device belongs to
the destination workspace: the API filters on it, the console shows it there,
and permissions follow. `transfer.adoption` answers the other question.

<TypeTable
  type={{
    confirmed: {
      type: "adoption",
      description:
        "The device has reported that it is publishing under the new tenancy. The only positive proof.",
    },
    pending: {
      type: "adoption",
      description:
        "It has not reported yet. The NORMAL immediate answer to a successful transfer, and the answer for every offline device. Not an error.",
    },
    unknown: {
      type: "adoption",
      description:
        "The device has never reported a telemetry scope at all, so there is nothing to compare. An agent older than v0.0.6 never sends one.",
    },
  }}
/>

Three values, never two. `unknown` means _never told us_, which is not the same
claim as _not yet scoped_, and collapsing it into `pending` would leave an old
agent wearing a "catching up" badge forever.

<Callout type="warn" title="`configRev` is NOT evidence that a transfer landed">
  A scope change deliberately does **not** bump the device's config revision, so
  `reportedConfig.rev` reads exactly the same before and after a transfer.
  Forcing a revision bump to provoke a republish is actively harmful — it makes
  the cloud re-kick the device on every reconnect.

For the same reason, "the device is still publishing" proves nothing either.
AWS IoT resolves a policy's thing-attribute variables at **connect** time, so
an established session keeps its old resolution and can keep publishing
happily for minutes after its tenancy changed. `adoption` is the only honest
signal.

</Callout>

## Two degraded outcomes worth handling

Both come back as `200`, so a `200` is not uniformly clean.

**`scopeAttributes: "failed"`** means the device moved and was told its new
topic, but the broker was not told to allow it. The device falls back to the
legacy telemetry plane, which is still ingested, so history and the console keep
working — but a workspace-scoped vended credential sees nothing until the
attributes are repaired. Saving any setting on the device re-asserts them.

**`adoption: "unknown"` on a fielded device** is your signal that its agent
predates `v0.0.6` and has no fallback to the unscoped telemetry topic. Such a
device can be left unable to publish until its agent process is restarted, which
needs physical or shell access. The API does not refuse the transfer — it
reports the condition. Update the fleet before transferring fielded hardware.

## Handing a device to an organization you are not in

A transfer needs authority in both the source and the destination, and a single
request resolves to exactly one organization. So there are two paths:

<Cards>
  <Card
    title="You administer both organizations"
    href="/getting-started/transfer-a-device"
    description="Pass organizationId and move it in one call. No handshake needed."
  />
  <Card
    title="You do not share a membership"
    href="/getting-started/hand-over-a-device"
    description="Mint an offer code, send it, and the destination redeems it."
  />
</Cards>

The offer is a genuine handshake: the source can withdraw it, the destination
can decline it and the source sees the decline, and it expires after 7 days.

<Callout title="There is no inbox of incoming offers, and there cannot be">
  An offer names no destination — not needing to know the recipient's
  organization id is the entire reason a code exists. So a pending offer is
  discoverable **only by its code**.

`GET /transfer-offers` lists what your organization SENT plus what it has
ACCEPTED, tagged by `direction`. A pending offer made _to_ you does not appear
in it and never will. Build a paste-a-code entry point, not a notification
list.

</Callout>
