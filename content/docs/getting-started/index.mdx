title: "What is xorgate"
description: "The platform, what it gives you, and how the pieces fit together."
---

Xorgate is an end-to-end platform for **Physical AI and Edge IoT**. It sits
between rugged sensing hardware in the field and the cloud workflows that make
sense of what that hardware sees, so product teams can ship sensor-driven
systems without rebuilding the hardware, connectivity, streaming, and AI
infrastructure every time.

## What the platform provides

- **Hardware kits.** Rugged sensing devices (cameras, GPS, accelerometer,
  environmental sensors) shipped as turnkey kits, with customization for
  bespoke form factors and sensor combinations.
- **SDKs.** First-party libraries for the **edge** (the device agent), the
  **cloud** (server-to-server integrations), and the **client** (web and mobile
  apps), so the same telemetry model flows end to end.
- **Video and IoT streaming.** Cellular-first, low-bandwidth-tolerant pipelines
  that move H.264 video and structured sensor data from device to cloud, and
  keep recording through dead zones.
- **A workflow-based AI layer.** Vision and sensor-fusion pipelines composed as
  templates (model, thresholds, alert rules) and run against live streams.

The core of that AI layer, the workflow definition format, the node catalog and
the engine that executes a template, is documented under
[Workflows](/workflows); its platform API is not yet. The rest of these docs cover
the core platform: the tenancy model, devices, telemetry, media, and the API and
SDKs you use to reach them.

## How the pieces fit together

<Steps>
<Step>

**Edge.** A device runs the xorgate agent. It collects telemetry once a second,
records video locally in roughly 60 second MP4 segments, and streams live video
peer to peer over WebRTC. It talks to the cloud over MQTT on a per-device X.509
identity.

</Step>
<Step>

**Cloud.** `xorgate-core-service` ingests telemetry, indexes uploaded media
segments, and exposes everything over a REST API. It also provisions devices:
minting their IoT identity and signaling channels.

</Step>
<Step>

**Client.** Your application reads the REST API for device state, telemetry
history, and recorded media, and connects directly to the live planes (MQTT for
telemetry, WebRTC for video) for real-time data.

</Step>
</Steps>

An important consequence of that last step: **the live planes bypass the REST
API.** Live telemetry and live video are direct browser-to-AWS connections that
the API only bootstraps. See
[Live versus recorded](/platform-concepts) for what that means in practice.
